So how does Spotify know what song you want?

ChatGPT plays one game: fill in the blank

When you type "The cat sat on the ___", ChatGPT's entire job is to guess the next word.

  • It looks at all the words before the blank.
  • It asks: "Based on these words, what word is most likely next?"
That's literally all it does. It fills in one blank. Then the sentence is longer, so it fills in the NEXT blank. Then the NEXT. One word at a time.
Write on the board: "The cat sat on the ___". Ask: "What word goes here?" Everyone says "mat." "How did YOUR brain know? You looked at 'cat', 'sat', 'on' and figured out what fits. We're going to learn that math.

But how? Four steps of arithmetic.

"The cat sat on the ___"

  1. Turn every word into a list of numbers. ("cat" → some numbers, ...)
  2. For each word, ask: how related is it to the blank? (Score each word.)
  3. Turn the scores into percentages. (cat 51%, sat 31%, on 11%, the 7%.)
  4. Mix the words' information using those percentages → predict "mat".
That's it. Four steps. Each step is a math concept we'll learn.
Write 4 steps on the board. "Step 1: REPRESENTATION — turn words into numbers. That's vectors. Week 1." "Step 2: COMPARISON — how related? That's the dot product. Week 1." Step 2: Measuring closeness with the dot product

Given two lists of numbers, multiply matching entries and add up:

"cat" \(= (4, 1)\), "dog" \(= (4.5, 2)\):

\[4 \times 4.5\; +\; 1 \times 2\; =\; 18 + 2\; =\; 20 \quad \leftarrow \textbf{big = very similar!}\]

"cat" \(= (4, 1)\), "democracy" \(= (0, 0)\):

\[4 \times 0\; +\; 1 \times 0\; =\; 0 \quad \leftarrow \textbf{zero = not related}\]

20 vs 0. The math confirms what you already felt: cat and dog are similar; cat and democracy are not.

This is middle-school arithmetic (multiply and add). But it's the single most important operation in all of AI.
DO THIS ON THE BOARD. Slowly. "4 times 4.5 is 18. 1 times 2 is 2. Add them: 20. Big number. Cat and dog are similar." "4 times 0 is 0. 1 times 0 is 0. Add: 0. Cat and democracy have nothing in common." Ask: "Could you do this? It's multiplication and addition. Middle school math." "This middle-school arithmetic is EXACTLY what ChatGPT does trillions of times. The dot product is not fancy math. It's multiply and add. But it's the single most important operation in all of AI."

Why does multiply-and-add measure similarity?

  • Both words score high on "animal" → product is a big positive (18).
  • Both words score low on "action" → product is small positive (2).
  • One high, other zero → product is zero (no agreement).

The dot product adds up all the agreements. More agreement → bigger number → more similar.

This is how ChatGPT decides which words are related. It computes this for every pair. That operation is called \(QK^\top\).
"The dot product adds up how much two words AGREE on every quality. Both animal-like? Big contribution. Both action-like? Also contributes. One high, one zero? Zero contribution." "The deep intuition: the dot product measures how much two vectors POINT IN THE SAME DIRECTION. We'll make this precise in Unit 1." Math → Real World: this simple arithmetic lets Spotify find similar songs, Google find similar pages, ChatGPT find related words.

Step 3: From scores to percentages

The blank "___" has its own numbers too: \(q = (0.5,\ 1)\). Score every word against it — same multiply-and-add as Step 2:

WordIts numbersMultiply and addScore
The\((0,\ 1)\)\(0(0.5) + 1(1)\)1
cat\((4,\ 1)\)\(4(0.5) + 1(1)\)3
sat\((1,\ 2)\)\(1(0.5) + 2(1)\)2.5
on\((1,\ 1)\)\(1(0.5) + 1(1)\)1.5

Bigger score = more related to the blank. But what does a score of 3 mean? We need percentages.

"The blank has its own report card too. To score each word, do exactly what we just did: multiply matching entries, add them up." "cat scores 3, the highest. sat 2.5. on 1.5. The barely 1." "But is 3 a lot? You can't tell. That's the next slide."

Step 3, continued: scores become percentages

The function that does this is softmax. It first grows each score, then divides by the total:

WordScore \(s\)Grow it: \(e^{s}\)Share of the total
The12.727%
cat320.0951%
sat2.512.1831%
on1.54.4811%
39.47100%

"cat" scores only twice what "on" does — but gets 4.5× the attention. Growing the scores first is what makes the winner win clearly.

"Softmax does two things. First it GROWS each score — 3 becomes about 20, 1.5 becomes about 4.5. Then it divides each by the total, 39.47, so everything adds to 100%." "Why grow them first? Watch: cat's score is exactly twice on's score. But cat ends up with 51% and on with 11% — four and a half times as much, not twice. Growing the scores first exaggerates the gaps, so the best word wins clearly instead of by a nose. That's what 'soft-max' means: a soft version of just picking the maximum." Expect a student to check this with ratios and think it's wrong — that is the right instinct, and this is the answer. "We do this properly in Week 12."

You all know how GPA works:

  • ChatGPT does the same: relevant words get more "credits" (attention).
This weighted average is called a linear combination — the central operation of linear algebra. (Units 1–2)
EVERY student knows GPA. "Your GPA is not a simple average. Math counts 4 times as much as Art. That's a weighted average." Do the arithmetic: 3.5 × 4 = 14, 3.0 × 3 = 9, 4.0 × 1 = 4. Total = 27. Divide by 8 = 3.375. "Middle school arithmetic." Attention is literally a GPA of words. Weights = how many credits each word gets. Output = weighted mix of word information. This is a linear combination — the first topic of Unit 1.

How does the machine learn? Like learning to cook.

Imagine you're learning to cook rice:

  1. First try: too much water. Mushy. Use less water.
  2. Second try: too little water. Crunchy. Use a bit more.
  3. Third try: almost right. Slightly dry. Tiny bit more.
  4. Eventually: perfect rice. You learned by adjusting one thing at a time.

ChatGPT learns the same way — except it adjusts billions of numbers instead of one.

"Try, fail, adjust, repeat. Each time, change ONE thing and see if it gets better. ChatGPT has 175 billion numbers to adjust. It makes a prediction, checks if it's right, figures out WHICH numbers to change and by HOW MUCH. Then again. Billions of times." "The math for 'which direction to adjust' is the GRADIENT (calculus). The algorithm for 'keep adjusting' is GRADIENT DESCENT (Weeks 5-10)." "But the core idea is simple. Try, fail, adjust. You know this from cooking, sports, any skill. The math just makes it precise."

The math of "try, fail, adjust"

  1. Try: The model predicts. ("Next word is 'dog'.")
  2. Fail: Measure how wrong. (Answer was "mat", not "dog".)
  3. Adjust: Calculus tells us which numbers to change and by how much.
"How wrong" → loss function (Week 2).
"Which direction" → gradient (Weeks 5–7).
"Keep adjusting" → gradient descent (Weeks 8–10).

By Week 10, you'll compute a gradient by hand and watch a model learn.

"Measuring 'how wrong': the loss function. Simplest = Mean Squared Error — difference squared. Week 2." "Which direction: the gradient. A list that says 'increase #1, decrease #2...' Weeks 5-7." "Keep adjusting: gradient descent. Walk downhill on the error surface. Weeks 8-10." "Calculus isn't about abstract curves — it's about LEARNING. Every time ChatGPT improves, calculus told it which direction to go." AI → Math motivation: "I want to understand how AI LEARNS. For that, I need calculus."

The attention formula — now you know what it means

\[\text{Attention}(Q, K, V) = \text{softmax}\!\left(\frac{QK^\top}{\sqrt{d_k}}\right)V\]

Read it using what we just learned:

  1. \(QK^\top\) → Dot product between every pair of words. (How related?)
  2. \(\div\sqrt{d_k}\) → Scale down so numbers don't get extreme.
  3. softmax → Convert scores to percentages.
  4. \(\cdot\; V\) → Take the weighted average.
This is the complete recipe. Everything else in ChatGPT is repetition and plumbing.
"You can now READ this formula. An hour ago it was hieroglyphics. Now you know what Q, K, V are, what the dot product does, what softmax does, what ·V does." "We haven't done the math rigorously — that's the semester. But you know the STORY. In math, knowing the story is half the battle." Point at each piece on the board as you read it.

Why the \(\div\sqrt{d_k}\)?

Without scaling: scores \(= [40,\ 32,\ 24]\) → \([99.97\%,\ 0.03\%,\ 0.00\%]\)

With scaling \((\div\sqrt{d_k}=\div 8)\): scores \(= [5,\ 4,\ 3]\) → \([67\%,\ 24\%,\ 9\%]\)

  • Without scaling: essentially 100% on ONE word. Can only "look at" one thing.
  • With scaling: spreads attention across several words. Much better.

Like a student who reads one chapter vs. several — the second understands better.

"When vectors are long (768 dimensions), dot products sum 768 terms → huge numbers → softmax goes near-100% or near-0%." "That's bad: model can only attend to ONE word. Understanding requires MULTIPLE words." "Dividing by √d_k brings scores to a reasonable range. Like adjusting headphone volume — too loud, you hear only the loudest instrument. Turn it down, you hear the whole band." We'll make this precise in Unit 1 (norms).

One problem, five ways

We'll solve the same problem five times: fit a line to data.

  1. Geometry (Wk 2): drop a perpendicular.
  2. Algebra (Wk 3): solve a system of equations.
  3. Calculus (Wk 5): find where the error slope is zero.
  4. Optimization (Wk 8): start anywhere, walk downhill.
  5. Probability (Wk 13): find the line that best explains randomness.

Five approaches, same answer every time. Each teaches something the others can't.

"Each approach gives a different INSIGHT. Geometry shows SHAPE. Calculus shows SLOPE. Probability shows NOISE. Like looking at a building from five angles — no single view is complete." "Payoff: understanding regression five ways means understanding HOW ChatGPT learns. ChatGPT learning = regression at scale — fitting a model to data by walking downhill on the error."
